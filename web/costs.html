Assessing the modeled alternatives

A qualifying row has a 95% Wilson lower bound of at least 90% for the selected target, within this model. This threshold does not validate assumed cycle times, reliability or costs.

Where the shift cost goes

Average allocated shift cost, using the selected assumption case. Labor includes a dedicated rework operator in every configuration.

What the calculation includes

  • Eight paid hours per operator, including planned nonproduction time.
  • Equipment, fixtures, controls, integration, commissioning, training and installation.
  • Deployment contingency, maintenance, energy, consumables and incremental scrap losses.
  • Capital spread over a stated useful life and operating schedule.

What the result can tell us

Conversion cost compares the modeled assembly work. It excludes good-unit materials, premises, general overhead, taxes and financing.

Demand-capped cost charges the full simulated shift but gives no value to output above demand. It does not simulate reduced staffing or a shorter shift.

No payback is estimated: a defensible annual cash benefit has not been established.

All prices and process inputs are assumed. Low/base/high cases reuse the same production runs; they describe cost sensitivity, not statistical confidence. Walking, unloading and constrained maintenance resources remain omitted. View complete assumptions and evidence ↗
